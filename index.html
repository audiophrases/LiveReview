Whose games are we watching?

Play on Chess.com as you always do. The moment a game ends, it opens here already being reviewed — board, advantage bar and a verdict on every move — while you still remember what you were thinking.

Why after the game, and not during it?

Chess.com does not show a game in progress to outside apps, and that is on purpose. An advantage bar or a "that was a mistake" during your own game is engine help under Chess.com's Fair Play rules, even with no best move on screen. A bar that jumps to +4 after your opponent's move tells you there is a winning shot. So the review arrives the instant the game is over, which is the earliest point it can be fair.

Every move in the review shows the engine's top three moves from the position it was played in, with the move actually played marked among them. That is a fair way to learn from a move once the game is over. During a game it is not: the best moves you could have played last turn, and what each was worth, tell you what the position on the board holds now.

For feedback move by move while you play, practise against the engine, where help is allowed: Play and Spar in the bar above both have an advantage bar.
